*Creating Illusions: Practical Approaches to Teaching “Added Value” in Audiovisual Artworks*

Take a moment to study this image. What do you notice and how would you describe what is happening here?

请花一点时间仔细观察这张图。你注意到了什么？你会如何描述这里正在发生的事情？
 
![巴西船队 5](/media/music-pedagogy/roust-image01.jpg) [*“Brazilian flotilla 5,” uploaded by Vinicius1 to stock.XCHNG*](http://www.sxc.hu/photo/727959).

[*“巴西船队 5”，由 Vinicius1 上传至 stock.XCHNG*](http://www.sxc.hu/photo/727959).

Without any context, of course, there are any number of things that could be happening. If we imagine a story for the image, we might say:

当然，在没有任何语境的情况下，可能正在发生的事情数不胜数。如果我们为这张图设想一个故事，也许会这样说：

“The first two planes are breaking away from the other two.”

“头两架飞机正在脱离另外两架。”

“The last plane has almost caught up to the rest of the group.”

“最后一架飞机几乎要追上队伍中的其余飞机了。”

“Something is wrong with the last plane! It’s falling behind!”

“最后一架飞机出了问题！它掉队了！”

“What happened to the fifth plane?”

“第五架飞机怎么了？”

In each case, the addition of language brings new, richer meaning to the static image of four planes. It enhances, shapes, perhaps even distorts what we see in the image. That these sentences create meaning is a result of what film sound theorist [Michel Chion (1994, 5)](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL12156324W/Audio-vision) calls “_audiovisual illusion_, an illusion located first and foremost in the heart of the most important of relations between sound and image...what we shall call _added value_.”

在每一种情形中，语言的加入都为这幅由四架飞机组成的静止影像带来了新的、更丰富的含义。它强化、塑造，甚至可能扭曲我们在影像中看到的东西。这些句子之所以能够创造含义，正是电影声音理论家[米歇尔·希翁（Michel Chion，1994，5）](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL12156324W/Audio-vision)所称的“_视听错觉_，一种首先且最主要地坐落于声音与影像之间最重要的关系核心之中的错觉……即我们所谓的_附加价值_”。

I have taught film music courses as both semester-long offerings at Oberlin College and as 6-week summer courses at Roosevelt University. At Oberlin, the classes generally consisted of a balanced mix of music majors and non-music majors, with only the occasional film major. At Roosevelt, all of the students (so far) have majored in subjects other than music and film studies. Regardless of the institution or the students’ majors, however, I find that most of them come into the course believing that certain music is “right” for a given film scene. What they are initially reluctant to accept is that there is always more than one “right” music for that scene. Indeed, the perception of music and images together shapes the meaning of the audiovisual scene, so that the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

我曾在欧柏林学院开设过整学期的电影音乐课程，也曾在罗斯福大学开设过为期 6 周的暑期课程。在欧柏林，班级通常由音乐专业与非音乐专业学生均衡混合构成，偶有电影专业学生。在罗斯福，所有学生（迄今）的主修专业都是音乐与电影研究之外的领域。然而，无论院校或学生的主修专业如何，我发现多数学生进入这门课程时都相信某种音乐对某个给定的电影场景而言是“正确”的。他们起初不愿接受的是：对该场景而言，从来都不止一种“正确”的音乐。事实上，音乐与影像的合观共同塑造了视听场景的含义，因此整体大于各部分之和。

To demonstrate this point, I begin the course by playing a 25-second, stock-footage video of military jets: 

为了说明这一点，我在课程开始时播放一段 25 秒的军用喷气机影像素材视频：

<iframe src="https://player.vimeo.com/video/71516784" width="100%" height="400" frameborder="0" allow="autoplay; fullscreen; picture-in-picture" allowfullscreen></iframe> *[Added Value Clip (Colin Roust)](http://vimeo.com/71516784) from [FlipCamp](http://vimeo.com/user19882083) on [Vimeo](http://vimeo.com).*

*[附加价值片段（Colin Roust）](http://vimeo.com/71516784)，作者 [FlipCamp](http://vimeo.com/user19882083)，发布于 [Vimeo](http://vimeo.com)。*

Without sound, the clip could be anything, much like the photo at the beginning of this essay. But once music is added, the students quickly put the clip into a larger (imagined) narrative. For example, when they see the planes and hear [Kenny Loggins’s “Danger Zone,”](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yK0P1Bk8Cx4) the students describe the pilots as hot shots or “bad boy” heroes, much like Tom Cruise’s Maverick or Val Kilmer’s Ice Man in [Top Gun](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0092099/). With [Dmitri Tiomkin’s song “Mission Accomplished,”](http://youtu.be/1XHD2O_A7EM?t=3m10s) from [The Guns of Navarone](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0054953/), the students describe the jets as having just won a battle. Philip Glass’s [“Pruitt Igoe,”](http://grooveshark.com/s/Pruitt+Igoe/2gQc63?src=5) a cue from [Koyaanisqatsi](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085809/), generates tension for most students; the pilots are waiting, anticipating something. [“Running One,”](http://grooveshark.com/s/Running+One+Johnny+Klimek+Reinhold+Heil+Tom+Tykwer/4dDBh5?src=5) from Tom Tykwer’s score for [Run, Lola, Run](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0130827/) generates a different kind of tension. Some of the fighter pilots are now perceived as menacing, threatening villains.

没有声音时，这段影像可以是任何东西，很像本文开头那张照片。但一旦加入音乐，学生便会迅速把这段影像置入一个更宏大的（想象出的）叙事之中。例如，当他们看到飞机并听到 [Kenny Loggins 的“Danger Zone”](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yK0P1Bk8Cx4)时，学生会把飞行员描述为神气十足的人物或“坏小子”式英雄，很像[《壮志凌云》](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0092099/)中汤姆·克鲁斯饰演的 Maverick 或瓦尔·基尔默饰演的 Ice Man。配上 [Dmitri Tiomkin 的歌曲“Mission Accomplished”](http://youtu.be/1XHD2O_A7EM?t=3m10s)——出自[《纳瓦隆大炮》](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0054953/)——学生则把这些喷气机描述为刚刚赢得一场战斗。菲利普·格拉斯的[“Pruitt Igoe”](http://grooveshark.com/s/Pruitt+Igoe/2gQc63?src=5)，即[《机械生活》](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0085809/)中的一段提示音乐，令多数学生感到紧张；飞行员正在等待，期待着什么。汤姆·提克威为[《罗拉快跑》](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0130827/)所作配乐中的[“Running One”](http://grooveshark.com/s/Running+One+Johnny+Klimek+Reinhold+Heil+Tom+Tykwer/4dDBh5?src=5)则产生另一种紧张感。现在，部分战斗机飞行员被感知为凶险、具有威胁性的反派。

This exercise encourages students to experience the significance of added value. No musical cue is inherently, essentially “right” for a given image. Rather, somebody _chose_ to combine this music with that image. The apparent “rightness” or “naturalness” of that artistic choice shapes our perception and, ultimately, guides our interpretation and understanding of what is happening.

这一练习让学生亲身体验附加价值的意义。没有任何一段音乐提示对某个给定的影像而言是本质上、天然“正确”的。相反，是有人_选择_把这段音乐与那个影像组合在一起。这一艺术选择表面上的“正确性”或“自然性”塑造了我们的感知，并最终引导我们对正在发生的事情作出解读与理解。

To reinforce this exercise, I have the students face this kind of artistic choice from a creative perspective by completing a Silent Film Scoring Project. The project is introduced as early as possible in the semester, during the second class meeting or the second week, so that students can apply their new “insider” experience of film and their awareness of the audiovisual illusion to analyses and discussions throughout the remainder of the course. In smaller-sized classes, students work alone; in larger classes, students work in three-person production teams, with one student as the director (responsible for the artistic vision of the project), one as music supervisor (responsible for selecting appropriate music to realize the director’s vision), and one as editor (responsible for creating the final cut of the film). Depending on the length of the term students have either one or two weeks to complete the project.

为强化这一练习，我让学生通过完成一个“无声片配乐项目”，从创作的角度面对这类艺术选择。该项目在学期中尽可能早地引入，安排在第二次课或第二周，以便学生在课程余下的分析工作中，能够运用他们对电影新获得的“内部人”体验，以及对视听错觉的觉察。班级人数较少时，学生独立完成；班级人数较多时，学生组成三人制作团队，其中一人任导演（负责项目的艺术构想），一人任音乐监制（负责挑选合适的音乐以实现导演的构想），一人任剪辑（负责完成影片的最终剪辑）。视学期长度不同，学生有一到两周时间完成该项目。

### Learning objectives:｜学习目标：

1.  Gain familiarity with basic video editing software.／熟悉基本视频剪辑软件。
2.  Gain familiarity with basic principles of video and audio editing.／熟悉视频与音频剪辑的基本原则。
3.  Explain three strategies for scoring films: composition, improvisation, and compilation.／解释为电影配乐的三种策略：原创作曲、即兴演奏与编配辑录。
4.  Recognize and evaluate the variety of solutions possible when scoring a film.／认识并评估为电影配乐时可能存在的多种解决方案。
5.  Apply basic concepts of audiovisual analysis in discussion and a short comparative analysis paper.／在课堂讨论与一篇简短的比较分析论文中运用视听分析的基本概念。

### Materials needed｜所需材料

I provide each student/team with one blank DVD and with a copy of a film that is in the public domain. One-reel films from the nickelodeon era work especially well, since they tend to be between 5 and 10 minutes long. To facilitate comparative analysis, multiple students/production teams are assigned the same film. Finally, because my students have had little to no experience with music and video editing, I also lead a one-hour workshop on basic video editing software (Windows Movie Maker or iMovie).

我为每位学生／每个团队提供一张空白 DVD，以及一部已进入公有领域的影片的拷贝。镍币影院时代的一卷本影片效果尤佳，因为它们通常时长在 5 到 10 分钟之间。为便于比较分析，多名学生／多个制作团队会拿到同一部影片。最后，由于我的学生在音乐与视频剪辑方面经验极少甚至全无，我还会主持一场一小时的讲习，讲解基本视频剪辑软件（Windows Movie Maker 或 iMovie）。

### Method｜方法

The project is introduced with a discussion of scoring strategies based on Fred Karlin’s [_Listening to Movies_ (1994, 78–84)](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL3918007W/Listening_to_movies): playing the drama, hitting the action, playing through the action, phrasing the drama, and playing the psychological subtext. In addition to discussing concepts such as spotting and sync points, we focus on the three strategies used to create scores during the silent era: composing an original score, improvising an accompaniment, and compiling a score from any pre-existing music of their choice. Students are free to select any of these strategies, though any improvised scores must be recorded and synchronized on the finished DVD, rather than performed live in class. The prompt for the project follows the standard procedure for producing commercial films:

项目的引入以一场关于配乐策略的讨论开始，依据弗雷德·卡林（Fred Karlin）的[*Listening to Movies*｜《聆听电影》（1994, 78–84）](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL3918007W/Listening_to_movies)：演奏剧情、点中动作、贯穿动作演奏、为剧情分句，以及演奏心理潜台词。除讨论“标记”（spotting）与同步点等概念外，我们还聚焦无声时代用于创作配乐的三种策略：创作原创配乐、即兴伴奏，以及从他们自选的任何既有音乐中编配辑录成配乐。学生可自由选择其中任何一种策略，但任何即兴配乐都必须在完成的 DVD 上录制并同步，而不能在课堂上现场演奏。项目任务说明遵循商业影片制作的标准流程：

#### Production phase｜制作阶段

Already completed when the students are handed the fine cut of the film (edited images without any sound).

在把影片精剪版（已剪辑、无任何声音的影像）交给学生时，这一阶段已经完成。

#### Spotting session｜标记会议

While watching the film, each student/team decides what kind of music to use for each cue, when each cue should begin and end, what sync points they want to emphasize, and whether or not to include any sound effects or dialogue. (The sound effects and dialogue are not explicitly encouraged, but are not prohibited.) At this point, the student/team creates the cue sheet to use as an editing script.

在观看影片时，每位学生／每个团队决定每段提示使用何种音乐、每段提示应在何时开始与结束、希望强调哪些同步点，以及是否加入音效或对白。（音效与对白并未被明确鼓励，但也不被禁止。）此时，学生／团队制作提示单（cue sheet），用作剪辑脚本。

![提示单模板](/media/music-pedagogy/roust-image00.png)*Cue Sheet Template distributed as a Microsoft Excel file*.

*以 Microsoft Excel 文件形式分发的提示单模板*。

#### Post-production｜后期制作

Using basic video editing software, the student/team creates and adds a soundtrack to their film. If any changes to the plan are made, the cue sheet must be revised to reflect the final cut of the film.

学生／团队使用基本视频剪辑软件，为自己的影片制作并添加音轨。若计划有所更改，必须修订提示单，以反映影片的最终剪辑。

#### Distribution｜发行

Using DVD authoring software (such as iMovie or Windows DVD Maker), the student/team creates a finished DVD of their film.

学生／团队使用 DVD 制作软件（如 iMovie 或 Windows DVD Maker），制作影片的成品 DVD。

#### Exhibition｜展映

Each film is screened in-class. The student/team gives a short presentation—no longer than five minutes—about the creative decisions made in creating their score. Discussion after each screening is focused on the clarity and execution of the artistic vision, and on a comparison with the other scores created for the same film.

每部影片都在课堂上放映。学生／团队就其配乐创作中所作的创意决策作简短陈述——不超过五分钟。每次放映后的讨论聚焦于艺术构想的清晰度与执行情况，以及与该影片其他配乐作品的比较。

### Final Product｜最终成果

Each student/team submits a finished DVD of their now-synchronized-sound film and a simple cue sheet for their score. When students work in a production team, I also have them write a short, three-to-five-page analysis paper comparing their score to their colleagues’ scores for the same film. As an example, a business major with no background in film studies or music courses chose to create a compiled score using various pieces of classical music: 

每位学生／每个团队提交一张已完成、声音已同步的影片 DVD，以及一份简要的配乐提示单。当学生以制作团队形式工作时，我还要求他们撰写一篇三到五页的简短分析论文，把自己的配乐与同事为同一部影片所作的配乐进行比较。举一个例子：一位没有任何电影研究或音乐课程背景的商科专业学生，选择用若干古典音乐作品编配辑录成配乐：

With the length and slower pacing of the cues, the student sought to balance the faster activity of the images, giving a greater sense of continuity to each stage in the plot’s development. In contrast, a psychology major in the class aimed for an “authentic” nickelodeon feel by using “old timey” piano music:

通过提示段落的时长与较慢的节奏，这名学生试图平衡影像中较快的活动，为情节发展的每个阶段赋予更强的连贯感。与之相反，班上一位心理学专业学生则通过使用“老式”钢琴音乐，追求一种“地道”的镍币影院感受：

The editing of this student’s score had faster pacing and more sync points, responding to key moments in each scene. This student also relied on a contrast of major and minor modes to suggest the inherent innocence of the beggar and the evilness of the hand.  

这名学生的配乐剪辑节奏更快，同步点更多，以呼应每个场景中的关键时刻。这名学生还借助大调与小调的对比，来暗示乞丐与生俱来的天真以及那只手的邪恶。

Every time that I assign this project, I am consistently pleased and excited by the creativity and critical thought demonstrated by my students. In one particularly memorable case, four production teams had been given the same scene from [_White Shadows in the South Seas_](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0019574), at the arrival of Sebastian’s ship arrives on an island in the South Pacific. The scene begins with the islanders gathering on the beach as Sebastian is rowed ashore by his men. Dr. Lloyd sits on the beach, clearly unhappy. The two angrily confront each other and eventually come to blows. When one of Sebastian’s sailors shoots Lloyd, the islanders run in panic. In the chaos, Lloyd finds the chief’s daughter and dies in her arms. One of the student scores used cliché melancholy and love cues to focus our attention on Dr. Lloyd, the marooned, alcoholic doctor who had fallen in love with an indigenous girl and who feared that the arrival of whites would ruin the island paradise. Another score contrasted romantic-era orchestral music with percussion-dominated pieces to emphasize the indigenes as primitive and savage. Yet another used cues from a variety of action and war films to emphasize Sebastian’s crew as ruthless invaders and the indigenes as sympathetic victims. Due to an overabundance of sync points, this score made the scene feel like a Keystone Kops-style comedy. The final score consisted entirely of popular songs that had been selected based on a perceived link between the action on screen and the song’s title or lyrics. Taken as a whole, these scores became a vehicle for introducing students to the concept of style topics, or _topoi_, an essential feature of most mainstream film music.

每次布置这个项目，我都会持续地因学生展现出的创造力与批判性思考而感到欣喜与兴奋。其中一个特别令人难忘的例子是：四个制作团队拿到了[《南海白影》](http://www.imdb.com/title/tt0019574)中的同一场景，即塞巴斯蒂安的船抵达南太平洋某座岛屿之时。场景开始于岛民在沙滩上聚集，塞巴斯蒂安由手下划桨送上岸。劳埃德医生坐在沙滩上，显然很不高兴。两人愤怒地对峙，最终动起手来。当塞巴斯蒂安的一名水手开枪击中劳埃德后，岛民惊慌奔逃。混乱之中，劳埃德找到了酋长的女儿，并在她怀中死去。其中一份学生配乐使用了老套的忧郁与爱情提示段，把我们的注意力引向劳埃德医生——这位被放逐、酗酒的医生爱上了一位原住民姑娘，并担心白人的到来会毁掉这座岛上天堂。另一份配乐把浪漫主义时期的管弦乐与以打击乐为主导的乐曲相对照，以强调原住民的原始与野蛮。还有一份配乐使用了来自各种动作片与战争片的提示段，以强调塞巴斯蒂安的手下是残暴的入侵者，而原住民则是令人同情的受害者。由于同步点过多，这份配乐让整个场景感觉像是一出启斯东警察式的喜剧。最后一份配乐则完全由流行歌曲组成，这些歌曲是依据银幕动作与歌曲标题或歌词之间被感知到的关联而选出的。总体而言，这些配乐成为一个载体，把学生引向风格主题（style topics），即 _topoi_ 这一概念——多数主流电影音乐的一个根本特征。

Throughout both of these exercises, students learn fundamental concepts of film music by disrupting the traditional audiovisual experience. By exposing the artificialness of film music, the added value exercise helps students evaluate a given film’s score in the context of what choices the filmmakers did _and did not_ make.  This makes the students more keenly and critically aware of what effects the filmmakers sought to create and how effective those creative decisions are. The Silent Film Scoring Project reinforces this by giving the students practical, hands-on experience with these types of creative decisions. In their projects, the students synthesize course concepts and defend their creative decisions. By beginning the semester with these two experiences, the students encounter all of the films in the remainder of the course through the lens of these fundamental questions: What is happening in this scene? How does the sound enhance, shape, or distort what is happening? In other words, what audiovisual illusion is being created by sound and images, and what added value is created by that illusion?

在这两项练习中，学生都通过打破传统的视听经验来学习电影音乐的基本概念。通过揭示电影音乐的人为性，附加价值练习帮助学生在“电影制作者作出了_以及没有作出_哪些选择”这一语境中评估某部影片的配乐。这使学生更敏锐、更具批判性地觉察到电影制作者试图创造何种效果，以及这些创意决策的有效程度。无声片配乐项目则通过让学生亲手实践这类创意决策，进一步强化这一点。在项目中，学生综合运用课程概念，并为自己作出的创意决策辩护。以这两项体验作为学期开端，学生在课程余下时间里观看每一部影片时，都会带着这样几个基本问题：这个场景中正在发生什么？声音如何强化、塑造或扭曲正在发生的事情？换言之，声音与影像正在制造何种视听错觉，而这一错觉又创造了何种附加价值？
