*Using Audacity to Participate in Active Musical Listening*

A common refrain among music theory instructors is that some students don’t actively listen to the compositions assigned as out-of-class activities. Without engaged listening, students often return to class with jumbled, logically inconsistent, or even “unmusical” interpretations. In my experience teaching an upper-level Form and Analysis course, students did not translate the attentive, engaged listening modeled in the classroom to their take-home analysis assignments. For instance, in class I can direct students to attend to certain features while following the score, guide students through the composition without the score using animations or other visual cues, or ask students to physically respond to particular musical events (e.g., raise your hand when you hear a PAC, the return of the opening theme, etc.). Even with directions focusing students’ attention to specific musical details on take-home analysis assignments, students were unable to support their interpretations in class, which in turn stifled class discussion. Of course, teaching students to be engaged listeners is not a new problem ([a recent textbook by Matthew Santa](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL13780382W/Hearing_form), which admirably integrates aural and score analysis, is just one of many resources available), so I developed course materials for an upper-level Form and Analysis course (taught Fall 2012) that used the free and open-source digital editing software [Audacity](http://audacity.sourceforge.net/) to teach independent, active listening habits.

音乐理论教师中常见的一种抱怨是，有些学生不会主动聆听布置为课外活动的作品。缺乏投入的聆听，学生回到课堂时往往带着混乱、逻辑不一致甚至“不音乐”的解读。以我教授高年级曲式与分析课的经验来看，学生并未把课堂上示范的那种专注、投入的聆听迁移到课后分析作业上。例如，在课堂上我可以引导学生在看谱的同时关注某些特征，借助动画或其他视觉提示在无谱的情况下带领学生听完作品，或要求学生针对特定音乐事件作出身体反应（例如听到完全正格终止、开头主题回归时举手等）。即便课后分析作业中的指示已将学生的注意力聚焦到具体音乐细节上，学生仍无法在课堂上支持自己的解读，进而抑制了课堂讨论。当然，教学生成为投入的聆听者并不是一个新问题（[Matthew Santa 最近出版的一本教材](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL13780382W/Hearing_form)很好地整合了听觉分析与乐谱分析，只是众多可用资源之一），因此我为高年级曲式与分析课（2012 年秋季开设）编写了一套课程材料，使用免费开源的数字编辑软件 [Audacity](http://audacity.sourceforge.net/) 来教授独立、主动的聆听习惯。

An instructor could adopt Audacity as an intermediary between aural analysis and score analysis no matter the pedagogical approach, the choice of textbook, or the theoretical perspective on form. (Other software could be used with these activities, for instance, ([Variations Audio Timeliner, supported by Indiana University](http://variations.sourceforge.net/vat/index.html).) For this particular class, we used James Mathes’ [The Analysis of Musical Form](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL7947089W/The_Analysis_of_Musical_Form). I chose this text because of its emphasis on goal-directed motions, formal functions, and textural changes, all events that students could easily identify aurally. Further, this text incorporates both aural and written activities, and some of the activities described below are modifications of the aural assignments found in this textbook.

无论采用何种教学取向、选用哪本教材、持何种曲式理论视角，教师都可以把 Audacity 用作听觉分析与乐谱分析之间的中介。（这些活动也可以使用其他软件，例如（[印第安纳大学支持的 Variations Audio Timeliner](http://variations.sourceforge.net/vat/index.html)。）就这个具体的班级而言，我们使用 James Mathes 的 [The Analysis of Musical Form](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL7947089W/The_Analysis_of_Musical_Form)。我选择这本教材是因为它强调目标导向的运动、曲式功能与织体变化，这些事件都是学生能够轻易通过听觉辨认的。此外，这本教材同时纳入听觉活动与书面活动，下文描述的部分活动就是对这本教材中听觉作业的改写。

For this course, the main learning objective was for students to be able to communicate and support effectively their analysis of a composition in writing and in speaking while recognizing other possible interpretations. Also, since some of my students still struggled with basic aural skills (some were not able to distinguish aurally between a half and full cadence), I needed to create learning activities that encouraged critical thinking and aural analysis, and remediated deficient aural skills. In order to meet these goals, students completed weekly listening assignments delivered online through our learning management system (LMS); these listening activities prepared the weekly analysis assignments, which integrated aural and score analysis. I incorporated Audacity-based activities in both types of assignments as well as within the classroom.

这门课的主要学习目标是让学生能够以书面和口头方式有效表达并支持自己对一部作品的分析，同时能够识别其他可能的解读。此外，由于我的部分学生仍在基础听觉技能上挣扎（有些学生无法在听觉上区分半终止与完全终止），我需要设计既鼓励批判性思维与听觉分析、又能补救欠缺听觉技能的学习活动。为达成这些目标，学生每周完成通过我们的学习管理系统（LMS）在线发布的聆听作业；这些聆听活动为每周的分析作业作准备，而分析作业整合了听觉分析与乐谱分析。我在两类作业以及课堂教学中都纳入了基于 Audacity 的活动。

Over the course of the semester, students completed ten listening assignments designed to assess aural skills and vocabulary comprehension. All the assignments contained questions (multiple choice/answer, matching, or fill-in-the-blank) that referred to unambiguous musical examples. These were graded through the LMS, allowing students to receive immediate feedback. Students had the opportunity to repeat each listening activity up to three times, and their last grade was the score for the assignment. The first few assignments focused on the aural identification of cadences, tonal areas, phrase types, and formal functions. By the end of the semester, students were aurally identifying formal sections and structural events in large forms (sonata and sonata-rondo).

整个学期中，学生完成了十次聆听作业，用以评估听觉技能与术语理解。所有作业都包含针对无歧义音乐示例的问题（选择题/简答题、配对题或填空题）。这些作业通过 LMS 评分，使学生能够获得即时反馈。学生最多可以把每次聆听活动重复做三遍，最后一次的成绩即为该作业的分数。前几次作业侧重于听觉上辨认终止式、调性区域、乐句类型与曲式功能。到学期末，学生已能在听觉上辨认大型曲式（奏鸣曲式与奏鸣回旋曲式）中的曲式段落与结构事件。

[Karpinski 2000](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL5736751W/Aural_Skills_Acquisition) describes two types of activities which would focus listening on form: answering a set of guided questions in prose and creating some sort of a visual representation, like a chart or a diagram, of the composition. Since I wanted students to have immediate feedback and the opportunity to take the assessment multiple times, these types of activities would not be practical. To get around this problem, I used Audacity as a visual scaffold. For most assignments, students downloaded an .mp3 file and .txt file and imported both files into Audacity. (For more detailed information regarding importing and exporting labels in Audacity, refer to the [Audacity wiki](http://wiki.audacityteam.org/wiki/Labels_Tips).) While the same types of questions could be easily answered by giving the students a series of time points, as [Alegant (2008)](http://jmtp.ou.edu/journal-article/listen-thoughts-ipods-sonata-form-and-analysis-without-score) describes in his article about analyzing sonata form without a score, the visual representation of the sound wave allowed students to see the larger sections dictated by textural, dynamic, and thematic changes. In anonymous evaluations of this class, a few students reported that watching the cursor move across the sound wave allowed them to pick up on thematic repetitions more quickly and focused their attention on the music. Another advantage to using Audacity for this type of listening assignment is that students can take notes in the label track as the composition unfolds, and repeated listenings can confirm their initial thoughts about the composition.

虽然仅给出学生一系列时间点也能轻易回答同类问题——正如 [Alegant (2008)](http://jmtp.ou.edu/journal-article/listen-thoughts-ipods-sonata-form-and-analysis-without-score) 在其关于无谱分析奏鸣曲式的文章中所描述的——但声音波形的视觉呈现使学生能够看到由织体、力度与主题变化所决定的大段落划分。在这门课的匿名评价中，有少数学生反映，看着光标在声波上移动使他们更快捕捉到主题的重复，并把注意力集中在音乐上。使用 Audacity 进行这类聆听作业的另一个优势是，学生可以在作品展开的过程中在标签轨道上做笔记，而反复聆听可以印证他们最初对作品的判断。

The example below shows an in-class activity used in preparation for the listening assignment on sonata-form expositions (Mozart, Symphony No. 40, the exposition and its repeat). Using vocabulary from our text, students were asked to describe what part of the sonata exposition was taking place at each numerical label and to support their answer with musical details. The entire activity was completed without the aid of a score; the labels in Audacity allowed us to communicate effectively about sections of the piece. While the crux of this activity focused on aural events that signified formal sections (cadences, thematic and textural changes), this activity also modeled how to support an analysis.

下面的例子展示了为一堂关于奏鸣曲式呈示部的聆听作业（莫扎特《第四十交响曲》，呈示部及其反复）作准备而使用的课堂活动。学生被要求用我们教材中的术语描述每个数字标签处正在发生奏鸣曲式呈示部的哪个部分，并用音乐细节支持自己的答案。整个活动在没有乐谱辅助的情况下完成；Audacity 中的标签使我们能够就作品的各个段落进行有效沟通。这一活动的核心虽然聚焦于标示曲式段落的听觉事件（终止式、主题与织体变化），但它也示范了如何支持一项分析。

In another set of Audacity-based activities students created their own label tracks as a preliminary step to a score analysis. There are two ways to create a marker on a label track in Audacity: you can place a label at the beginning of a selection (where you first positioned the cursor) or at the current playback position. This latter option can be used as the music plays, and so it allows for students to create a real-time analysis, marking specific musical features for future reference. For students who aren’t strong score readers, this allows them to notate an aural analysis, and then annotate the score at their leisure.

在另一组基于 Audacity 的活动中，学生自行创建标签轨道，作为乐谱分析的预备步骤。在 Audacity 中，有两种方式在标签轨道上创建标记：你可以在选区的开头（即你最初放置光标的位置）放置标签，也可以在当前播放位置放置标签。后一种方式可以在音乐播放时使用，因而允许学生进行实时分析，把特定的音乐特征标记下来以备后续参考。对于不擅长读谱的学生而言，这使他们能够记下一次听觉分析，然后再从容地对乐谱加以批注。

In one class activity, I asked students to actively anticipate the arrival of harmonic goals, predicting the onset of the last chord of a cadential pattern by placing a label at the onset of that chord as the composition unfolds. This activity focused listening on cadential arrivals supporting the goal-directed awareness advocated by our textbook. This attentiveness to harmonic goals and cadential articulations is the foundation of recent formal theories taught in the class, notably [Hepokoski and Darcy’s (2006)](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL4294182W/Elements_of_sonata_theory) theories on sonata form. Other activities, where students mark motivic repetitions, thematic returns, textural changes, etc., could also allow students to experience theoretical concepts in a tangible way. By turning theoretical concepts into an aural experience, students better retained knowledge and accurately applied these concepts to independent analyses.

在一次课堂活动中，我要求学生主动预期和声目标的到来，在作品展开的过程中，通过在某个终止式型态最后一个和弦出现的位置放置标签来预测该和弦的出现。这项活动把聆听聚焦于终止式的到达，支持我们教材所倡导的目标导向意识。这种对和声目标与终止式清晰化的关注，是课上讲授的晚近曲式理论的基础，尤其是 [Hepokoski 与 Darcy（2006）](http://openlibrary.org/works/OL4294182W/Elements_of_sonata_theory) 关于奏鸣曲式的理论。其他活动——例如让学生标记动机重复、主题回归、织体变化等——也能让学生以可感的方式体验理论概念。通过把理论概念转化为听觉经验，学生更好地记住了知识，并把这些概念准确地应用于独立分析之中。

I used a similar activity as a part of a few analysis assignments. As a precursor to score-based analysis, students created a label track where they identified and labeled cadences. They then exported the label track as a .txt file, which they submitted electronically prior to class. While students could have used the score to help identify the cadences, the act of labeling the audio file encouraged students to rely on their ears and musical intuitions. The sound was the starting point for analysis, not the score. As a second step, I asked the students to label sections and structural events also within Audacity. The text quoted below shows the preliminary instructions to an early sonata form assignment. I gave the students a list of possible events in a sonata form, and they decided if and when they occur. Labeling within Audacity allowed students to quickly reference sections they marked as similar and compare these sections to confirm their initial analysis. Following this preliminary aural analysis, students annotated their score and supported their analysis using musical details in prose (usually answering a set of questions or writing a two-page mini paper).

我在若干次分析作业中也使用了类似的活动。作为基于乐谱的分析之前的铺垫，学生创建一条标签轨道，在其中辨认并标记终止式。随后他们把标签轨道导出为 .txt 文件，并在课前以电子方式提交。尽管学生本可以利用乐谱来帮助辨认终止式，但为音频文件加标签这一行为鼓励学生依靠自己的耳朵与音乐直觉。声音是分析的起点，而非乐谱。作为第二步，我要求学生在 Audacity 中也标记段落与结构事件。下文引用的文字是某次早期奏鸣曲式作业的初步指示。我给学生一份奏鸣曲式中可能出现的事件清单，由他们判断这些事件是否发生以及何时发生。在 Audacity 中加标签使学生能够快速调出他们标记为相似的段落，并对这些段落加以比较以确认自己最初的分析。在这次初步的听觉分析之后，学生对乐谱加以批注，并用文字以音乐细节支持自己的分析（通常是回答一组问题或写一篇两页的短文）。

From a practical standpoint, having text files from each student for cadences and sections allowed me to evaluate an individual’s analysis prior to class discussion. By importing each student’s analysis into Audacity, I could quickly spot conceptual misunderstandings, and it gave me a clear reference to the student’s analysis, which may be clouded by unclear prose. The Audacity-based activities described in this essay can teach students to actively attend while listening to music, to aurally identify structural and thematic cues for formal analysis, and to support an analytic interpretation with musical details. Further application of this program could explore ways in which to connect theoretical ideas with the listening experience. A range of activities could take advantage of label tracks in creating a real-time analysis; activities which allow students to experience theoretical ideas, connecting these ideas with the musical act of active listening. By encouraging student engagement in the preliminary listening experience prior to a score analysis, these activities can foster a deeper understanding of a composition: students are able to support their reading, resulting in fruitful and active class discussions.

本文所述的基于 Audacity 的活动可以教学生聆听音乐时主动投入，在听觉上辨认用于曲式分析的结构与主题提示，并用音乐细节支持分析性解读。该程序的进一步应用可以探索把理论想法与聆听经验相联结的方式。一系列活动都可以利用标签轨道来创建实时分析；这些活动让学生体验理论想法，并把这些想法与主动聆听的音乐行为联系起来。通过鼓励学生在乐谱分析之前投入初步聆听经验，这些活动能够培养对作品更深入的理解：学生能够支持自己的解读，从而带来富有成果的、活跃的课堂讨论。
