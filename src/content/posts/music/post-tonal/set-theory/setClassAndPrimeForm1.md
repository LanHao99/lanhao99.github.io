Lots of concepts in pitch-class set theory are best viewed along a sliding scale of “concreteness” or “abstractness.” A concept like *pitch*, for example, is very concrete, while *pitch class* is somewhat more abstract. We can perform a pitch, but we can’t really perform a pitch class. We’ve seen similar examples in the intervallic realm. Ordered pitch intervals are associated with a very specific sound (e.g., +15); unordered pitch-class intervals (e.g., interval class 1) are less vivid or real. A basic concept in pitch-class set theory is that these levels of concreteness and abstractness encompass not only pitch and interval, but groups of pitch classes as well. These groups of pitch classes are called *pitch-class sets*.

音级集合理论中的许多概念，最好沿着一条“具体”与“抽象”的连续尺度来看待。例如，*音高*这样的概念非常具体，而*音级*则较为抽象。我们可以演唱或演奏一个音高，却无法真正演唱或演奏一个音级。我们在音程领域已见过类似的例子。有序音高音程与一种非常具体的音响相联系（例如 +15）；无序音级音程（例如音程类 1）则不那么鲜明或真实。音级集合理论的一个基本观念是：这些具体与抽象的层级不仅涵盖音高与音程，也涵盖音级的组。这些音级的组称为*音级集合*。

We’ve already seen sets of pitch-classes, though we haven’t really been calling them that. When we extract a group of notes from a passage of music and put them in [normal order｜标准序](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/normalOrder/), that group of notes is a pitch-class set. As we’ve seen in class, one very interesting way of looking at a lot of post-tonal music is by studying the [transpositional｜移位](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/transposition/) and [inversional｜倒影](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/inversion/) relationships between pitch-class sets. In the short example below (from Bartók’s “Subject and Reflection”) you’ll notice that the right hand of the two passages is *T5*-related, as is the left-hand. Within each passage, the right and left hands are *T8I \_and \_T6I* related, respectively.

我们其实已经见过音级的集合，只是没有这样称呼它们。当我们从一段音乐中抽出若干音并使其处于[标准序](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/normalOrder/)时，这一组音就是一个音级集合。正如课堂上所见，考察大量后调性音乐的一种非常有趣的方式，是研究音级集合之间的[移位](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/transposition/)与[倒影](/posts/music/post-tonal/set-theory/inversion/)关系。在下面这个简短的例子（选自巴托克《主题与反射》）中，你会注意到两个片段的右手声部呈 *T5* 关系，左手声部也是如此。在每一片段内部，右手与左手分别呈 *T8I 与 T6I* 关系。

*In order for a pitch-class set to be transpositionally or inversionally related to some other pitch class set, they must share the same collection of intervals*. This is most easily grasped by remembering that all major and minor triads have the same interval content (M3, m3, and P5). Major triads are transpositionally related to one another, while major and minor triads are inversionally related to one another. The same observation applies in Bartók’s “Subject and Reflection.” The four pitch-class sets in those two passages all have the same intervallic content and that’s why we can label transpositional and inversional relationships between them.

*要使一个音级集合与另一个音级集合呈移位或倒影关系，它们必须含有相同的音程集合*。最容易把握这一点的方法是记住：所有大三和弦与小三和弦都具有相同的音程内容（大三度、小三度与纯五度）。大三和弦彼此呈移位关系，而大三和弦与小三和弦彼此呈倒影关系。同样的观察也适用于巴托克的《主题与反射》。那两个片段中的四个音级集合都具有相同的音程内容，这正是我们能够标示它们之间移位与倒影关系的原因。

All pitch-class sets that are transpositionally and inversionally related belong to the same *set class*, and they are represented by the same *prime form*. We follow a simple process to put a pitch-class set in prime form:

所有彼此呈移位与倒影关系的音级集合都属于同一个*集合类*，并由同一个*本原形式*来代表。我们依照以下简单步骤把一个音级集合化为本原形式：

1. Put the pitch-class set in normal order.／把该音级集合化为标准序。
2. Transpose it so that the first pitch class is 0.／对它进行移位，使第一个音级为 0。
3. Invert the results from step 2 (any inversion will work) and put the result in normal order.／把第 2 步的结果加以倒影（任何倒影都可以），并把结果化为标准序。
4. Transpose it so that the first pitch class is 0.／对它进行移位，使第一个音级为 0。
5. Compare the results of steps (2) and (4). Prime form is the most compact version.／比较第（2）步与第（4）步的结果。最紧凑的那个版本就是本原形式。
