*The lament progression*

**||: I – VII – VI – V :||**

This progression need not be included in a cycle, but occasionally it does. It is named the “lament” progression because in early classical music, this chord progression (almost always in minor) was used as the *ground bass* (a repeated bass pattern that formed be foundation for a set of variations, not unlike the cyclical progressions of pop/rock songs) for songs of lament. Examples include “Dido’s Lament” by Henry Purcell, from the opera *Dido and Aeneas*, and J.S. Bach’s “Crucifixus,” from his Mass in B Minor.

这一进行不必被纳入循环之中，但偶尔它确实如此。它被称为“哀歌”进行，是因为在早期古典音乐中，这一和弦进行（几乎总是出现在小调中）被用作哀歌类歌曲的*固定低音*（一种反复出现的低音模式，构成一组变奏的基础，与流行／摇滚歌曲的循环进行颇为相似）。例子包括 Henry Purcell 歌剧《Dido and Aeneas》中的《Dido’s Lament》，以及 J.S. Bach《b 小调弥撒》中的《Crucifixus》。

The opening of the verse in Muse’s “Thoughts of a Dying Atheist” is a prominent example from recent pop/rock music (it is followed by a [circle-of-fifths progression](/posts/music/pop-rock/harmony/popRockHarmony-fifths/)).

Muse 的《Thoughts of a Dying Atheist》中主歌的开头是近期流行／摇滚音乐中一个突出的例子（其后接一个[五度圈进行｜circle-of-fifths progression](/posts/music/pop-rock/harmony/popRockHarmony-fifths/)）。
